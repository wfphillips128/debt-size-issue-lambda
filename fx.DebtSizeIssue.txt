/* ==========================================================================
   fx.DebtSizeIssueλ                    Advanced Formula Environment source
   --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Sizes a brand-new serial debt issuance. Par is an OUTPUT, not an input:
   each period's maturity is sized to the coverage test, and the tranches so
   created are what generate interest.

   Covers steps 1-5 of the Project work list:
     1) no opening balance needed
     2) DBTRSVPCT / DBTRSVRATE live - reserve sized off MAXIMUM ANNUAL debt
        service and its earnings credited against debt service
     3) no initial debt balance required
     4) a coupon CURVE - a different rate on each tranche (taxable case)
     5) par-value rounding at $1, $1,000 or $5,000

   COUPON IS A PER-TRANCHE RATE.
   COUPON(y) is the coupon on the tranche MATURING in year y - not "the rate
   applied to the balance in year y". Interest owed in any year is the sum of
   the coupons on every tranche still outstanding, which includes maturities
   far beyond that year. When every coupon is identical the two readings are
   algebraically the same, which is why a flat 5% never exposes the difference.
   With a rising coupon curve, balance-times-one-rate is wrong by a material
   amount every year, and it changes sign - overstating the early years and
   understating the late ones.

   So this function does not derive interest from the balance. It carries the
   REMAINING ANNUAL COUPON and retires each tranche's coupon as that tranche
   matures:

       Interest(y)  = CoupRem(y) * MONTHS(y) / 12
       Principal(y) = FLOOR(MAX(0, CFADS(y)/DSCR(y) - Interest(y) + Credit(y)),
                            ParValueIncr(y))
       CoupRem(y+1) = CoupRem(y) - Principal(y) * COUPON(y)

   THE PAYOFF DATE IS A HARD CONSTRAINT, NOT A COLUMN COUNT.
   The tranche ladder is generated off the delivery date and runs a fixed
   number of columns wide. The date the issue must be retired by does not
   move with it. Push the delivery date out and the far end of the ladder
   walks past the payoff date, so the columns handed to this function stop
   being all-live: the last few are tranches that may not legally exist.

   Two things then go wrong if the function just trusts its column count.
   It sizes maturities into those dead columns; and - the larger error - it
   releases the debt service reserve at Idx = Periods, the last column passed,
   rather than at the last LIVE maturity. The release is what funds the big
   final tranche. Stranded in a dead column it funds nothing, the reserve
   itself is sized off the wrong maximum annual debt service, and the whole
   issue is mis-sized. Worse, if a dead column carries no revenue at all the
   release is the only cash in it, so the function invents a maturity there
   with negative coverage.

   MATURITY and LastPayoffDate close that. Live = MATURITY <= LastPayoffDate;
   principal is sized only where Live, reserve earnings accrue only where Live,
   and the reserve is released at the last live period. Omit both and the
   function falls back to "live wherever CFADS is positive", which reproduces
   the previous behaviour exactly when every column carries revenue.

   SOLVER NOTE.
   The Lance Rubin pattern (feed the previous iteration's payment forward) is
   fixed-point iteration. It converges when the opening balance is GIVEN. Once
   the issue size is unknown it diverges: raising the balance raises interest,
   which cuts principal by more than the balance rose. Verified - 200
   iterations, no convergence. This function bisects instead, on TOTAL ANNUAL
   COUPON, which is unconditionally stable because the residual is monotonic.

   WHICH SIDE OF THE BRACKET YOU STOP ON IS A FEASIBILITY QUESTION.
   Par rounding means the residual never reaches zero, so the bisection ends
   straddling the answer rather than on it. The two sides are not equally
   valid. The low side sizes every maturity assuming less interest than the
   issue really carries, which puts par one increment too high and lands
   coverage just under the test - 1.09999x against a 1.10 covenant, invisible
   behind a one-decimal display. The high side assumes slightly more interest
   than the issue carries and is feasible in every period. So the terminal
   step rebuilds on the HIGH bracket, never on the last probe.

   THE CEILING HAS TO BE A REAL CEILING - AND REVENUE ALONE IS NOT ONE.
   Everything above depends on HiB genuinely bracketing the answer from
   above. The first ceiling used here was total live revenue over the
   coverage ratio, times the highest coupon - i.e. "par can never exceed the
   revenue that services it". That is false. Revenue is not the only thing
   that funds principal: the debt service reserve is RELEASED at the last
   live period and funds principal too. On a long ladder the release is
   small next to nine years of revenue and the bound holds by accident. On a
   SHORT ladder - a late delivery date leaving two or three live tranches -
   the release is a quarter of the whole issue, par exceeds revenue/DSCR,
   and the true answer sits ABOVE the ceiling. The bisection then never
   brackets it, pins at its own ceiling for all 50 steps, and returns an
   under-sized issue that quietly breaches the covenant.

   Worse, the pinned trial coupon is BELOW the coupon the maturities really
   carry, so CoupRem runs NEGATIVE once the last live tranche retires. A
   negative remaining coupon is a negative Intr, and Raw = -Intr is then
   POSITIVE in exactly the columns that are supposed to be dead: the
   function issues bonds maturing years past the payoff date. Observed with
   a flat coupon and a delivery date late enough to leave two live tranches
   - several percent of par maturing up to seven years past the payoff date,
   and coverage more than 100bp under the covenant. It fails quietly: par,
   sources and uses all still tie, because the schedule is internally
   consistent. Only the coverage row and the maturity dates give it away.

   Two changes close it, and they are independent:
     - HiB is now GROWN until its residual is actually negative. Step opens
       at -12 instead of 1, and while it is negative the recursion probes the
       ceiling and doubles it rather than bisecting - 4096x of headroom
       against an observed worst case of 1.09x. This reuses the recursion
       that already exists rather than adding a nested REDUCE, which would
       have stacked three iterator-LAMBDAs (REDUCE over Solveλ's own REDUCE
       over CoupRemλ's SCAN) and costs two extra levels, not 144 extra
       Partsλ evaluations. A seed that was already valid passes on the first
       probe, so every previously-correct answer is bit-identical.
     - Principal is multiplied by Live in BOTH CoupRemλ and Partsλ, so a
       dead column can never be sized whatever the sign of Intr. This is
       belt-and-braces once the ceiling is right, but it makes the invariant
       explicit rather than emergent.

   STORED LENGTH IS A HARD CONSTRAINT AT 8192 CHARACTERS.
   Excel caps a single formula at 8192 characters, and it measures the form
   it STORES, not the text you paste. Storage adds a prefix to every name:
   _xlfn. on post-2007 functions, _xlpm. on lambda parameters and LET names,
   _xlop. on optional lambda parameters, _xlws. on worksheet-only functions
   such as FILTER. On this function those prefixes alone are about 1950
   characters, so a 6200-character paste is stored at over 8100. Over the
   limit, .xlsx saves warn and Name Manager refuses the paste outright.

   Two consequences worth knowing before editing this:

     - A RECURSIVE CALL IS THE MOST EXPENSIVE THING IN HERE. Twelve
       arguments, every one prefixed, is 185 stored characters per call
       site. That is why the four branches of the solver are written as
       three expressions (NxtLo / NxtHi / NxtIt) feeding ONE call, rather
       than as four calls. It reads worse and it is not optional.

     - THE HELP TABLE IS THE LARGEST SINGLE BLOCK. String literals are the
       one part with no prefix overhead but they are counted in full. Long
       prose belongs in these comments, which AFE strips on import and which
       therefore cost nothing at all.

   If this ever does need to exceed 8192, saving the workbook as .xlsb
   (Excel Binary Workbook) lifts the limit - that is what the save-time
   warning is offering. Prefer shortening.

   A FLAT COUPON IS THE HARD CASE HERE, NOT THE EASY ONE.
   Only the flat-coupon issue ever showed this, and that is not luck. The
   ceiling uses MAX(COUPON) across ALL columns including the dead ones, and
   on a normal rising curve the dead far-end tranches carry the highest
   coupons on the curve - which inflates the ceiling by enough to bracket
   the answer by accident. A flat curve has no such slack, so the ceiling
   degenerates to exactly par-times-coupon with nothing spare. Test a
   coupon-curve function on a FLAT curve as well as a rising one; the flat
   case is where a bad bound has nowhere to hide.

   ========================================================================== */

/* FUNCTION NAME: fx.DebtSizeIssueλ
   DESCRIPTION:*//**Sizes a new serial issuance to a coverage test, with a per-tranche coupon curve, a MADS-based debt service reserve and a hard payoff date*/
/* REVISIONS: Date         Developer   Description
              Aug 07 2026  W Phillips  Initial release
              Aug 07 2026  W Phillips  Per-tranche coupons - interest is now
                                       carried as remaining annual coupon
                                       rather than derived from the balance
              Aug 09 2026  W Phillips  Renamed BXLDebt.SizeIssuanceλ to
                                       fx.DebtSizeIssueλ
              Aug 10 2026  W Phillips  MATURITY / LastPayoffDate added. Maturities
                                       past the payoff date are no longer sized,
                                       and the reserve is released at the last
                                       LIVE period, not the last column passed
              Aug 10 2026  W Phillips  Terminal step now rebuilds on the HIGH
                                       bisection bracket rather than the last
                                       probe. The last probe could land on the
                                       low side, which oversizes par by one
                                       increment and breaches the coverage test
              Aug 10 2026  W Phillips  The bisection ceiling is now GROWN until
                                       it actually brackets the answer. Live
                                       revenue / DSCR x max coupon is NOT an
                                       upper bound - the reserve release funds
                                       principal too, and on a short ladder par
                                       exceeds it. Step opens at -12 and the
                                       recursion doubles the ceiling until its
                                       residual goes negative. Principal is also
                                       Live-gated in CoupRemλ and Partsλ so a
                                       dead column cannot be sized even if Intr
                                       goes negative
              Aug 11 2026  W Phillips  Stored form was over Excel's 8192-char
                                       formula limit, which is why Name Manager
                                       refused the paste and .xlsx saves warned.
                                       The four solver branches now feed ONE
                                       recursive call via NxtLo / NxtHi / NxtIt,
                                       and the help table was tightened. No
                                       change to any number the function returns
*/
fx.DebtSizeIssueλ = LAMBDA(
    // ---- User arguments (all bracketed so the help table can display) ----
    [CFADS],          // Required. Cash flow available for debt service, per period
    [DSCR],           // Required. Debt service coverage ratio, per period
    [COUPON],         // Required. Coupon on the tranche MATURING in each period
    [DBTRSVPCT],      // Required. Reserve as a % of maximum annual debt service
    [DBTRSVRATE],     // Required. Earnings rate credited on the reserve
    [ParValueIncr],   // Required. Par rounding increment. Use 1 for none - never 0
    [MONTHS],         // Required. Months of interest accruing in each period
    [MATURITY],       // Optional. Maturity date of each period's tranche
    [LastPayoffDate], // Optional. Last date a maturity may fall on
    // ---- Internal state. Never passed by the user. ----
    [Lo],             // Bisection bracket, low
    [Hi],             // Bisection bracket, high
    [Iter],           // Bisection depth counter

    LET(
        // The help table is the single largest block of stored characters in
        // this function - see the STORED LENGTH note in the header. Keep it
        // terse. The long-form explanation belongs in these comments, which
        // cost nothing because AFE strips them on import.
        Help, TRIM(TEXTSPLIT(
            "DESCRIPTION:       →Sizes a new serial debt issuance to a coverage test¶" &
            "                   →Par is an OUTPUT - the sum of the sized maturities¶" &
            "SUGGESTED ROW IDS: →Debt opening balance / Interest / Principal repaid /¶" &
            "                   →Debt closing balance / Debt service / Reserve credit /¶" &
            "                   →Net debt service¶" &
            "VERSION:           →W Phillips, Aug-11-2026¶" &
            "PARAMETERS:→¶" &
            "CFADS              →(Required) Cash available for debt service, per period¶" &
            "DSCR               →(Required) Debt service coverage ratio, per period¶" &
            "COUPON             →(Required) Coupon on the tranche MATURING in each period¶" &
            "→ - a curve, not one rate on the balance. Flat is fine.¶" &
            "DBTRSVPCT          →(Required) Reserve as a % of MAXIMUM ANNUAL debt service¶" &
            "→ 25% is a 3-of-12-months reserve. The largest is used.¶" &
            "DBTRSVRATE         →(Required) Earnings rate credited on the reserve¶" &
            "ParValueIncr       →(Required) Par rounding increment. 1 = none. Never 0.¶" &
            "MONTHS             →(Required) Months of interest per period, eg. 10,12,12¶" &
            "MATURITY           →(Optional) Maturity date of each period's tranche¶" &
            "LastPayoffDate     →(Optional) Last date a maturity may fall on. Periods¶" &
            "→ after it are not sized; the reserve releases at the last live one.¶" &
            "Lo, Hi, Iter       →Internal - the bisection solver's state¶" &
            "→¶" &
            "EXAMPLE:           →=fx.DebtSizeIssueλ(M59#,M60#,M61#,M62#,M63#,M64#,M65#,M58#,PayoffDate)",
            "→", "¶")),

        ErrMsgs, {
            "CFADS must be a dynamic array holding cash available for debt service in each period";
            "DSCR must be a dynamic array holding the debt service coverage ratio for each period";
            "COUPON must be a dynamic array holding the coupon on the tranche maturing in each period";
            "DBTRSVPCT must be a dynamic array holding the reserve percentage for each period";
            "DBTRSVRATE must be a dynamic array holding the reserve earnings rate for each period";
            "ParValueIncr must be a dynamic array of par increments, every one greater than zero. Use 1 for no rounding";
            "MONTHS must be a dynamic array holding the months of interest accruing in each period";
            "MATURITY must be a dynamic array of maturity dates, the same width as CFADS"
        },

        // ---- Validate. The recursion passes these arrays through unchanged, so
        //      a recursive call never trips its own validation.
        CFADS,        IF(COLUMNS(CFADS) <= 1, #VALUE!, CFADS),
        DSCR,         IF(COLUMNS(DSCR) <= 1, #VALUE!, DSCR),
        COUPON,       IF(COLUMNS(COUPON) <= 1, #VALUE!, COUPON),
        DBTRSVPCT,    IF(COLUMNS(DBTRSVPCT) <= 1, #VALUE!, DBTRSVPCT),
        DBTRSVRATE,   IF(COLUMNS(DBTRSVRATE) <= 1, #VALUE!, DBTRSVRATE),
        ParValueIncr, IF(OR(COLUMNS(ParValueIncr) <= 1, MIN(ParValueIncr) <= 0), #VALUE!, ParValueIncr),
        MONTHS,       IF(COLUMNS(MONTHS) <= 1, #VALUE!, MONTHS),

        // ---- Resolve the payoff gate into two concrete values, so the recursive
        //      call can pass them positionally. ISOMITTED cannot be forwarded.
        //
        //      Supplied: Mat is the date row, Pay is LastPayoffDate, and Live
        //                is MATURITY <= LastPayoffDate as you would expect.
        //      Omitted:  Mat becomes --(CFADS <= 0) - 0 where there IS revenue -
        //                and Pay becomes 0, so Live is Mat <= 0, ie. revenue is
        //                positive. Both forms are idempotent: re-resolving Mat
        //                and Pay on the way down the recursion returns them
        //                unchanged, so every level sees the same mask.
        Mat,  IF(ISOMITTED(MATURITY), --(CFADS <= 0),
                 IF(COLUMNS(MATURITY) <> COLUMNS(CFADS), #VALUE!, MATURITY)),
        Pay,  IF(ISOMITTED(MATURITY), 0,
                 IF(ISOMITTED(LastPayoffDate), MAX(Mat), LastPayoffDate)),

        Errors, VSTACK(OR(ISERROR(CFADS)), OR(ISERROR(DSCR)), OR(ISERROR(COUPON)),
                       OR(ISERROR(DBTRSVPCT)), OR(ISERROR(DBTRSVRATE)),
                       OR(ISERROR(ParValueIncr)), OR(ISERROR(MONTHS)),
                       OR(ISERROR(Mat))),
        Messages, FILTER(ErrMsgs, Errors, ""),

        Periods, COLUMNS(CFADS),
        Idx,     SEQUENCE(1, Periods),
        RsvPct,  MAX(DBTRSVPCT),

        // ---- The payoff gate. Maturities ascend, so Live is a prefix mask and
        //      LastLive is simply its right-hand edge. All-dead gives 0, which
        //      matches no Idx, so nothing is released - which is correct, since
        //      an all-dead schedule sizes no debt and needs no reserve.
        Live,     --(Mat <= Pay),
        LastLive, MAX(Live * Idx),

        // ---- Reserve earnings credited in each LIVE period, plus the release of
        //      the balance itself at the last live period.
        Creditλ, LAMBDA(res,
            res * DBTRSVRATE * MONTHS / 12 * Live + res * (Idx = LastLive)),

        // ---- One forward pass, carrying the REMAINING ANNUAL COUPON. SCAN does
        //      the carry, so there is no recursion here. Each period sizes its
        //      maturity to the coverage test, then retires that tranche's coupon.
        //      Returns the coupon still outstanding AFTER each period.
        //
        //      Princ is multiplied by Live, not just Target. Gating Target alone
        //      leaves Target - Intr = -Intr in a dead column, which is POSITIVE
        //      whenever rem has gone negative, and mints a maturity there.
        CoupRemλ, LAMBDA(tc, res,
            LET(
                Cr, Creditλ(res),
                SCAN(tc, Idx, LAMBDA(rem, y,
                    LET(
                        Target, INDEX(CFADS, 1, y) / INDEX(DSCR, 1, y) * INDEX(Live, 1, y),
                        Intr,   rem * INDEX(MONTHS, 1, y) / 12,
                        Princ,  FLOOR(MAX(0, Target - Intr + INDEX(Cr, 1, y))
                                      * INDEX(Live, 1, y),
                                      INDEX(ParValueIncr, 1, y)),
                        rem - Princ * INDEX(COUPON, 1, y)
                    )))
            )),

        // ---- Principal and interest for a trial total coupon and reserve.
        //      MAX() would collapse an array to a scalar, so the floor at zero
        //      is written elementwise with IF(). Prin carries the same Live gate
        //      as CoupRemλ - the two must agree period for period.
        Partsλ, LAMBDA(tc, res,
            LET(
                Aft,  CoupRemλ(tc, res),
                Bef,  HSTACK(tc, DROP(Aft, , -1)),
                Intr, Bef * MONTHS / 12,
                Raw,  CFADS / DSCR * Live - Intr + Creditλ(res),
                Prin, FLOOR(IF(Raw < 0, 0, Raw) * Live, ParValueIncr),
                VSTACK(Prin, Intr)
            )),

        // ---- Terminal step. The issue actually sold carries the coupon implied
        //      by the maturities themselves, which the bisection probe only
        //      approaches - par rounding stops the residual reaching zero.
        //      Rebuild balances and interest on the true coupon, holding the
        //      maturities fixed, or every interest figure comes out overstated.
        //      Names avoid a trailing digit: Cl2, Op2, In2, Rsv2 and Cr2 are all
        //      valid cell addresses, and Excel rejects the whole binding list
        //      when a LET name looks like a reference.
        Finalλ, LAMBDA(parts,
            LET(
                Prin,   INDEX(parts, 1, 0),
                TrueTC, SUM(Prin * COUPON),
                Par,    SUM(Prin),
                Cum,    SCAN(0, Idx, LAMBDA(a, y, a + INDEX(Prin, 1, y))),
                ClEnd,  Par - Cum,
                OpEnd,  HSTACK(Par, DROP(ClEnd, , -1)),
                CumC,   SCAN(0, Idx, LAMBDA(a, y,
                            a + INDEX(Prin, 1, y) * INDEX(COUPON, 1, y))),
                RemBef, TrueTC - HSTACK(0, DROP(CumC, , -1)),
                IntEnd, RemBef * MONTHS / 12,
                RsvEnd, MAX(Prin + IntEnd) * RsvPct,
                CrEnd,  Creditλ(RsvEnd),
                VSTACK(OpEnd, IntEnd, -Prin, ClEnd, Prin + IntEnd,
                       -CrEnd, Prin + IntEnd - CrEnd)
            )),

        // ---- Bisection state. Omitted on the outer call. The bracket is on
        //      TOTAL ANNUAL COUPON, and HiB opens on live revenue over the
        //      coverage ratio times the highest coupon. That is a good GUESS
        //      and a bad BOUND, so Step opens NEGATIVE: a negative Step means
        //      "the ceiling has not been proved yet". See the Result block.
        LoB,  IF(ISOMITTED(Lo),   0,                                            Lo),
        HiB,  IF(ISOMITTED(Hi),   SUM(CFADS * Live) / MIN(DSCR) * MAX(COUPON),  Hi),
        Step, IF(ISOMITTED(Iter), -12,                                          Iter),
        Mid,  (LoB + HiB) / 2,

        // ---- Size the whole schedule at one trial total coupon. The reserve is
        //      sized off MAXIMUM ANNUAL debt service, which the reserve itself
        //      helps determine, so it is settled with REDUCE - not recursion,
        //      which keeps the bisection below the only recursion here.
        Solveλ, LAMBDA(tc,
            LET(Rsv, REDUCE(0, SEQUENCE(1, 12), LAMBDA(r, ignore,
                         LET(P, Partsλ(tc, r),
                             MAX(INDEX(P, 1, 0) + INDEX(P, 2, 0)) * RsvPct))),
                Partsλ(tc, Rsv))),

        // While the ceiling is unproved, probe the CEILING; once proved, probe
        // the midpoint as an ordinary bisection does.
        Grow,     Step < 0,
        Probe,    IF(Grow, HiB, Mid),
        Parts,    Solveλ(Probe),
        // The coupon the sized maturities actually create, against the trial.
        Residual, SUM(INDEX(Parts, 1, 0) * COUPON) - Probe,
        // Up = the answer is at or above the probe. In the GROW phase that
        // means the ceiling is still too low; in the BISECT phase it means
        // the lower half can be discarded. Same test, two readings.
        Up,       Residual >= 0,

        // ---- The next bracket, as three expressions rather than four call
        //      sites. This is not style: a recursive call costs 185 stored
        //      characters against a hard 8192 limit (see the header), and
        //      writing the four branches out longhand does not fit.
        //
        //        Grow &  Up   ceiling too low      -> Lo,  Hi*2, Step+1
        //        Grow & !Up   ceiling proved       -> Lo,  Hi,   1
        //        Bis  &  Up   answer in upper half -> Mid, Hi,   Step+1
        //        Bis  & !Up   answer in lower half -> Lo,  Mid,  Step+1
        NxtLo,    IF(Up, IF(Grow, LoB, Mid), LoB),
        NxtHi,    IF(Up, IF(Grow, HiB * 2, HiB), IF(Grow, HiB, Mid)),
        NxtIt,    IF(Up, Step + 1, IF(Grow, 1, Step + 1)),

        // ---- Bisect. Residual is monotonically decreasing in the trial coupon,
        //      so a non-negative residual means the answer is at or above Mid.
        //      Mat and Pay go down resolved, never as ISOMITTED.
        //
        //      TERMINATE ON HiB, NOT ON Mid. This is not tidying - it decides
        //      whether the schedule returned actually passes its own coverage
        //      test. Sizing uses the interest implied by the TRIAL coupon;
        //      Finalλ rebuilds interest on the coupon the maturities really
        //      create. Par rounding stops those two ever meeting, so the sign
        //      of Residual at the point we stop is what matters:
        //
        //        Residual > 0  true interest EXCEEDS what sizing assumed, so
        //                      every maturity was sized a notch too generously
        //                      and coverage comes in BELOW the test
        //        Residual < 0  true interest is BELOW what sizing assumed, so
        //                      the schedule is conservative and coverage holds
        //
        //      HiB only ever takes a value whose Residual was negative - which
        //      the GROW phase below now makes true of the opening ceiling as
        //      well. That was the missing half: this whole argument is only
        //      sound while HiB really is an upper bound, and a ceiling that
        //      never brackets leaves the bisection pinned on it for all 50
        //      steps with Residual > 0 - the breaching case, dressed as the
        //      safe one. LoB is the opposite invariant and is reliably one par
        //      increment too large. Mid is whichever side the 50th probe fell
        //      on - a coin toss decided in the 15th significant digit, which is
        //      why the same workbook could pass on one machine and breach on
        //      another. Verified: on the benchmark issue, LoB breaches at
        //      1.09999x against a 1.10 test while HiB clears it.
        //
        //      PHASE 1, Step < 0 - GROW. Probe is HiB itself. A negative
        //      residual proves HiB is above the answer, so hand the bracket
        //      straight to the bisection at Step 1; otherwise double the
        //      ceiling and come back. Twelve attempts is 4096x headroom
        //      against an observed worst case of 1.09x, and a seed that was
        //      already valid passes through on the first probe - which is why
        //      every previously-correct answer is unchanged to the cent.
        //
        //      PHASE 2, Step >= 0 - BISECT, exactly as before. Step < 0 can
        //      never satisfy Step >= 50, so the order of these tests is safe.
        Result, IF(Step >= 50,
                   Finalλ(Solveλ(HiB)),
                   fx.DebtSizeIssueλ(CFADS, DSCR, COUPON, DBTRSVPCT, DBTRSVRATE,
                                     ParValueIncr, MONTHS, Mat, Pay, NxtLo, NxtHi, NxtIt)),

        Return, IF(AND(Errors), 2, IF(OR(Errors), 3, IF(OR(ISERROR(Result)), 2, 1))),
        CHOOSE(Return, Result, Help, Messages)
    )
);

/* ==========================================================================
   OUTPUT ROWS
     1  Debt opening balance
     2  Interest on principal balance
     3  Principal repayments        (negative)
     4  Debt closing balance
     5  Debt service                (principal + interest)
     6  Debt reserve credit         (negative - reduces debt service)
     7  Net debt service            (row 5 + row 6)

   The output is always as wide as CFADS. Periods past the payoff date come
   back as zeros rather than being dropped, so the grid keeps its one-column-
   per-tranche mapping and no formula has to be re-pointed when the delivery
   date moves.

   HOW TO CALL IT
     One input block, one row per argument, all the same width. With the
     tranche maturity dates on the first row of the block and the payoff
     date in a single cell:

       =fx.DebtSizeIssueλ(CFADSrow, DSCRrow, COUPONrow, DBTRSVPCTrow,
                          DBTRSVRATErow, ParValueIncrRow, MONTHSrow,
                          MATURITYrow, PayoffDate)

     The MATURITY row is the same tranche ladder the grid matches its
     revenue against. Total par is not an argument - read it off row 1
     column 1 of the result, or as -SUM(row 3).

   WHY THE PAYOFF DATE MATTERS EVEN THOUGH PAR IS AN OUTPUT
     Because the reserve release is what funds the final maturity. Sized off
     maximum annual debt service, released in full at payoff, it makes the
     last tranche roughly a third larger than the ones before it. Put that
     release in the wrong column and two things move at once: the final
     tranche shrinks to a normal one, and maximum annual debt service - and
     therefore the reserve, and therefore every reserve credit in every
     period - is computed off a schedule that never existed.

   FEEDING IT A COUPON CURVE
     The COUPON row takes one coupon per tranche, not one blended rate: the
     rate on the maturity falling in each period, left to right. For a single-
     coupon issue enter the same rate the whole way across - the maths is
     identical in that case, which is why a flat coupon never exposes a
     balance-times-one-rate error.

   WHY THE GRID USES W:Y AND THIS FUNCTION DOES NOT
     In the grid, column X is each tranche's semiannual coupon (par x rate / 2)
     and column Y sums X from the current row down - the coupons on every
     tranche maturing on or after that date. That is the same quantity this
     function carries as CoupRem, just accumulated by lookback instead of by
     roll-forward. A spilling function cannot look down a column that does not
     exist yet, so it carries the total forward and retires each tranche's
     coupon as that tranche matures. Same answer, opposite direction.
